# Gather.io Customer Discovery Playbook

**Purpose:** run 7+ interviews without wasting them, pick one vertical, and get to 3 paid pilots.
**Status of product:** collection loop built, reminders / dashboard / export / multi-party not built. You are not demoing. You are diagnosing.
**Version:** 1.0, September 2026

**How to use this:**

1. Read Part 0 and Part 1 once, fully, before interview number one.
2. Read the relevant Part 3 brief in the 15 minutes before each call. Nothing else.
3. Fill the Part 5 capture sheet within 2 hours of finishing. Not the next day.
4. Run the synthesis gate in Part 5 after interview 5 and again after 10.

---

# PART 0: WHAT YOU ARE ACTUALLY TESTING

## 0.1 These 7 interviews are scouting, not validation

Your own kill condition is "3 paid live pilots from the first 20 to 25 qualified interviews **in the chosen vertical**." Seven interviews across seven different professions cannot produce that. Different job.

| Round | Who | Count | Question it answers | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Round 1 (now)** | These 7, plus 2 to 3 from Part 4 | 8 to 10 | *Which single profession has the most expensive version of this pain and can decide alone?* | One chosen vertical, one ranked backup |
| **Round 2 (next)** | Only the chosen profession | 15 to 20 | *Will three of them pay before the product is finished?* | 3 paid concierge pilots, or a kill |

Do not let Round 1 drift into Round 2. In Round 1 your job is to **disqualify** professions fast, not to sell to any of them. A great Round 1 outcome is discovering that four of these seven are dead ends, because that saves you two months.

One exception: if somebody in Round 1 spontaneously tries to buy or hand you a live case, take it. Never refuse a commitment because it's "the wrong round."

## 0.2 The eight hypotheses

Every question in this document exists to move one of these. Memorise the list. In the room, you are listening for evidence, not for agreement.

| # | Hypothesis | Counts as real evidence | Counts as a kill | Best tested by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H1** | External people will not create accounts, and this costs the requester real completion | They describe abandoning a portal, accepting WhatsApp photos because the client refused the portal, resetting passwords for clients | "My clients log into our portal fine, no issue" (and they can show you usage) | Insurance, Bank, CA |
| **H2** | Real cases involve several counterparties, each owing different things | They name 3+ distinct people or firms in one case, unprompted, and describe chasing them separately | Every case is one person, one list, always | Compliance consultant, Logistics, HR, DSA |
| **H3** | Rework (reject with reason, re-ask for exactly one item) is the expensive part | "They sent the wrong month", "blurry", "unsigned", "old version", and they can estimate how often | First submissions are almost always accepted as-is | CA, Licensing, Insurance claims |
| **H4** | Recurrence plus carry-forward is the differentiator | They re-ask the same person for documents they already hold, and they know it's stupid | Every case is one-time, with a new counterparty each time | CA, Licensing, Payroll compliance |
| **H5** | Validity and expiry turn this into a subscription-shaped need | They track expiry dates somewhere (usually a spreadsheet), and something bad happened when they missed one | Nothing they collect expires | Logistics, Compliance consultant, Insurance |
| **H6** | There is a named human whose job is partly chasing, and they cost money | They can name the person and estimate hours or salary | "It just happens", nobody owns it, zero hours | All |
| **H7** | One person can decide to buy, without procurement | They already pay for software personally or on a company card, and they name the price | "It would go to IT / head office / the board" | Licensing, CA, Logistics COO |
| **H8** | **They will enforce it as the default path** | They have already forced a process change on clients before and it stuck | "If a client insists on WhatsApp, I take it on WhatsApp, always" | All. This is the killer. |

**H8 is the one most founders never test.** A tool cannot stop chasing if the requester keeps accepting documents by WhatsApp whenever someone pushes back. Ask the H8 question in every single interview (it's in the Core spine below). If a profession fails H8 across the board, no feature saves you there.

## 0.3 The three structural bets, restated in customer language

You are not testing "do you like the idea." You are testing whether these three sentences make anyone lean forward:

1. **"One case, several people, each chased separately, closed only when every one of them is done."** (multi-party)
2. **"When you reject something, only that one item reopens, and the reason goes into the next reminder automatically."** (rework)
3. **"Next cycle, it never re-asks for what you already hold and is still valid."** (carry-forward)

Nobody will say these sentences to you. You have to hear their equivalents in the mess of what they actually describe. That translation is what Part 3 is for.

# PART 1: RULES OF THE ROOM

## 1.1 The one rule

**Ask about the past, never about the future.** Everything anyone tells you about what they *would* do, *might* pay, or *think is a great idea* is worthless. Everything they tell you about what they actually did last Tuesday is gold.

The whole interview is one move, repeated: *make them reconstruct a specific, recent, real instance, in detail, out loud.*

## 1.2 Opening scripts

### Cold (licensing consultant, compliance consultant, insurance, CA: they do not know a product exists)

> "Thanks for the time. I'm doing research on how [their profession] actually gets paperwork and information from people outside their own office. I'm not selling anything and I don't have anything to show you. I just want to understand how the work really happens, because everything written about it online is useless. Can I ask you to walk me through one recent, real example, start to finish? Nothing confidential, no client names needed."

Then shut up.

Two things that script does: it removes the pressure to be polite about an idea (there is no idea yet), and it sets the format (reconstruction, not opinion) in the first 30 seconds.

### Warm (bank manager, logistics COO, HR: they know you're building something)

> "You know I'm building something in this space, so let me set the rules: I'm deliberately not going to show it to you for the first half hour, because if I do, you'll start being helpful about my thing instead of honest about yours. I want to understand your actual process first. If there's time at the end, I'll show you 60 seconds of it and you can tell me where it would break."

"Tell me where it would break" is the single most useful framing you have. It gives them permission to criticise, which is the only feedback with information in it.

### If they ask "so what are you building?" in minute two

> "I'll tell you at the end, honestly. If I tell you now, you'll unconsciously shape your answers around it, and then I'll build the wrong thing. Ten more minutes on your side first?"

Nobody has ever been offended by this. It also signals competence.

## 1.3 Banned questions, and what to ask instead

Tape this to the top of your notebook.

| Never ask | Ask this instead |
| --- | --- |
| "Would you use this?" | "Walk me through the last time you did this." |
| "Would you pay ₹X for it?" | "What do you pay for today in this workflow, and to whom?" |
| "Is document collection painful?" | "How many times did you follow up on that last one? Over how many days?" |
| "Would a no-account link help?" | "How do clients actually send you documents today? Why that way?" |
| "Do you think this is a good idea?" | "Who else have you seen try to fix this? What happened to it?" |
| "How much time would this save?" | "Who does the chasing? How many hours a week does it take them? What do they cost?" |
| "Do you track expiry dates?" | "Show me where you'd look right now to find out what expires next month." |
| "Would your clients adopt it?" | "The last time you changed how clients send you things, what happened?" |
| "What features do you need?" | "What did you do the last time it went wrong? What did that cost?" |
| "Does this happen often?" | "How many did you do last month? And the month before?" |

Rule of thumb: if the answer to your question could be a compliment, it's the wrong question.

## 1.4 The Core spine (use in every interview, in this order)

Time budget: 45 minutes total. Roughly 3 / 20 / 8 / 6 / 5 / 3 across the six blocks.

### Block A. Locate them (3 min)

1. "What exactly is your role, and what part of this process is yours versus someone else's?"
2. "How many of these do you handle in a month? Was last month typical?"

Get to a number early. If the number is under 5 per month, the rest of the interview is culture, not commerce, and you should shorten it.

### Block B. Reconstruct one real case (20 min, the heart of it)

Pick their most recent completed one, not their worst one. Worst cases are war stories and are unrepresentative.

1. "What event kicked it off?"
2. "How did you know what to ask for? Is there a list, and where does it live?" *(probes: templates)*
3. "Who exactly did you have to get things from? Name the roles." *(probes: H2 multi-party)*
4. "How did you send the request? Show me the actual message if you still have it." *(ask for the artifact, always)*
5. "What came back first, and how complete was it?"
6. "What was wrong with what came back?" *(probes: H3)*
7. "How did you tell them it was wrong? What did you send them?"
8. "How many times back and forth, over how many days?"
9. "Who did the following up? You, or someone else?" *(probes: H6)*
10. "Where did the final documents end up? Walk me to the folder."
11. "Did anyone else inside your office have to check or approve them before it was done?" *(probes: reviewer separation)*
12. "How did you know it was finally complete? What told you?" *(probes: the dashboard need)*

Question 14 is underrated. If the answer is "I just knew" or "I opened the folder and counted", you have found the exact gap your dashboard fills.

### Block C. Repeat, carry-forward, expiry (8 min)

 1. "When the same client or vendor comes back next month or next year, what do you ask for again?"
 2. "Do you ever ask someone for something you already have on file? What happens then?" *(H4, listen for embarrassment, it's the strongest tell)*
 3. "Does anything you collect stop being valid after a while? How do you find out it expired?" *(H5)*
 4. "Show me where you'd look right now to see what's expiring next month."

### Block D. Cost (6 min)

 1. "What actually went wrong the last time one of these stalled? What did it cost?"
 2. "Is there a penalty, a fine, a lost deal, a delayed payment? Roughly how much?"
 3. "If you were away for two weeks, who would know what's pending and where?"

Then do the arithmetic **out loud, in front of them**:
> "So roughly: [N] follow-ups a month, each about [M] minutes, done by [person], plus [penalty] when it slips. That's around ₹[X] a month. Does that sound right, or am I over-counting?"

Saying the number back is the fastest way to get it corrected. A corrected number is a true number.

### Block E. Attempts, tools, and money (5 min)

 1. "What have you already tried to fix this? What happened to it?"
 2. "What software do you pay for today, for anything in this workflow? Roughly what does it cost you a year?" *(H7, this is your real price research)*
 3. "If you decided to buy a tool for this tomorrow, who has to say yes besides you?"

### Block F. The H8 question and the ask (3 min)

 1. **"If a client refused to use a new system and sent you documents on WhatsApp anyway, what would you do?"**

Answers to 25 and what they mean:

- "I'd tell them this is the only way now" → strong H8, this profession is enforceable.
- "I'd take it on WhatsApp, the client is the client" → weak H8. Note it. If a whole profession answers this way, deprioritise them.
- "Depends on the client" → probe: "which clients could you force, and how many of your clients are those?"

 1. Then the ask (see 1.7).

## 1.5 Numbers to leave with, every time

Do not end an interview without these written down. If one is missing, ask before you hang up.

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Cases per month | Below 5 per month, there is no deal |
| Parties per case | 1 vs 3+ decides multi-party priority |
| Items per case | Under 4 means Google Forms wins |
| Percentage accepted first time | This is your rework hypothesis in one number |
| Days from request to complete | Your headline improvement metric later |
| Follow-ups per case | Same |
| Who chases, and their rough cost per hour | Converts pain into rupees |
| Recurrence interval (monthly / quarterly / annual / never) | Decides carry-forward priority |
| Anything that expires? Y/N and what | Decides Phase 1.5 timing |
| Separate internal approver? Y/N | Validates `require_separate_reviewer` |
| Current tools, and annual spend | Your real price ceiling |
| Consequence of lateness, in rupees or in words | The qualifying question |

Useful cost anchors when they can't answer (state them as a question, not a fact): article / junior ₹100 to ₹250 per hour, ops executive ₹150 to ₹400, practice owner or COO ₹1,500 to ₹5,000.

## 1.6 Three behaviours in the room

**Silence.** After they stop talking, count three seconds before you speak. The second thing they say is the honest one. This is the single highest-return habit in interviewing and it feels unbearable the first four times.

**Compliments are a failure signal.** "This is a great idea", "you should definitely build this", "I'd love to try it" means you have learned exactly nothing and probably leaked your idea too early. Recover with: "That's kind, but tell me about the last time it actually went wrong instead."

**Ask for artifacts, not descriptions.** "Can you show me the actual message you sent?" "Can you forward me that checklist?" "Can I see the folder?" An artifact is evidence. A description is a memory, and memories are flattering.

## 1.7 The reveal protocol

For cold interviews, do not reveal before minute 35. When you do:

1. **Mirror, don't pitch.** "A few people I've spoken to described [specific thing they just told you]. Is that what you're dealing with too?"
2. **Then, one sentence only.** "I'm building something that handles exactly that part: one link per client, they don't create an account, it chases only what's still missing, and when you reject something only that one item reopens."
3. **Then hand them the knife.** "Where does that break for you? Where would it be useless?"

Never show the screen unless they ask twice. If they do, 60 seconds maximum, recipient page first (it is your best surface), then stop.

**Do not fix objections live.** When they say "it won't work because X", your only reply is "tell me more about X." The instant you say "oh, I can build that", the interview is over and you have started taking orders from a stranger.

## 1.8 The ask ladder: never end without an ask

Every interview must end with a request for something that costs them a little. Escalate until they say no. The level they stop at is your real signal, far more than anything they said.

| Level | The ask | What a yes proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Can you forward me the actual checklist / message you send?" | Mild interest, and you get a free template |
| 2 | "Can I sit with you, 20 minutes, the next time you do one of these?" | They believe you're serious |
| 3 | "Who else do you know doing this work? Would you introduce me to two of them?" | They're staking reputation, the strongest free signal |
| 4 | **"Your next real case: let me run the chasing for you manually, this month, free. You give me the list and the client's number, I do the rest."** | This is your concierge pilot. Real data through a real process. |
| 5 | **"₹[X] a month, starting with next month's cycle, while I'm still finishing the product."** | The only signal that counts |

Script for level 4, which is the one you want from Round 1:
> "Here's what would actually help me: next time you have one of these, don't change anything about your process. Just send me the list and the contact. I'll do the chasing myself, by hand, and send you the complete set. Free, no commitment, one case. If it's useless you never hear from me again."

That is a concierge pilot dressed as a favour, and it tests H8 without building anything.

## 1.9 Never say these

- "It's like DocuSign, but..." (you become a worse DocuSign in their head, permanently)
- "It's free right now." (kills the only measurement that matters)
- "I can build that for you." (you now owe a stranger a feature)
- "So you'd use this, right?" (fishing for the compliment that ruins the interview)
- "Most people I've spoken to say..." (you just told them the answer you want)

## 1.10 Logistics

- **45 minutes, ask for 30.** People give honest time to a 30-minute ask and then run over willingly.
- **Record, with permission:** "Mind if I record just for my own notes? Nothing gets shared, it's so I'm not typing while you talk." Almost everyone says yes. If they hesitate, drop it instantly and take notes.
- **No laptop, no deck.** Notebook and pen for in-person. For calls, camera on, no screenshare from your side until the reveal.
- **Let them use their own language.** If they switch to Hindi or Marathi, follow them. Do not translate their process into your product's vocabulary in the room. Do that afterwards, on the capture sheet.
- **Never use your words in their room.** Not "case", not "requirement", not "party", not "template". Use their words: file, packet, requirement (insurance), deficiency (bank), objection (licensing), PBC list (audit), docket. Your vocabulary is for your codebase, and using it makes them translate for you, badly.
- **Write the capture sheet within 2 hours.** After that you're recording your interpretation, not their answers.

# PART 2: WHAT ORDER TO DO THESE IN

The first two interviews you run will be your worst ones. Do not spend your best prospect on them.

| Slot | Who | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Freelance HR** | Warm, friendly, low stakes if you fumble it. Perfect rehearsal. |
| 2 | **Bank branch manager** | Warm, and the least likely to become a customer anyway, so a wasted interview costs little. Also gets the regulated-buyer objections into your head early. |
| 3 | **Chartered Accountant** | Your current planned wedge. You should be sharp by now. |
| 4 | **Licensing consultant** | Possibly your actual best wedge. Needs you at full sharpness. |
| 5 | **Logistics and transport COO** | Biggest potential cheque on the list, and the most complex process to hold in your head. |
| 6 | **Compliance consultant (BIS / TUV)** | Best multi-party test. |
| 7 | **Insurance** | Most likely to be a pure insight interview, least likely to convert. Safe to place last. |

If scheduling forces a different order, the only rule that matters: **do not put the licensing consultant or the CA first.**

Between interview 3 and 4, take a day. Re-read your first three capture sheets and rewrite your question list. Your Part 3 briefs are a starting point, not scripture. By interview 5 you should have three questions in every interview that came from something a previous person said.

## 3.1 Insurance company

### First, find out who you're actually talking to

"Insurance" is four different products. Your first question decides the whole interview:

> "Which side of the business are you on: selling and issuing policies, underwriting, claims, or onboarding agents and intermediaries?"

| Their side | What they collect, from whom | Value to you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Claims** | Bills, discharge summary, FIR, estimate, cancelled cheque, ID, from a distressed retail customer | **Highest.** Best H1 and H3 test anywhere on your list |
| **New business / underwriting** | Proposal form, KYC, medicals, income proof, from customer and agent | High. Multi-party (customer + agent + diagnostic centre) |
| **Agent / intermediary onboarding** | Licence, exam certificate, PAN, bank details, training records, with renewal cycles | High for H5 (expiry) |
| **Vendor / hospital empanelment** | Registration, agreements, rate cards | Medium |

If they are a corporate employee at a large insurer, accept that this is an **insight interview**, not a sale. Mine it for H1 and H3 and move on. If they are an **agency owner, broker, or third party administrator**, they can actually buy. Find out which in minute one.

### Their vocabulary (use theirs, listen for yours)

"Requirement" (they literally say *raise a requirement* and *requirement pending*, which is your product in their words), "deficiency", "pending documents", "query", "repudiation", "discharge voucher", "NEFT mandate", "KYC", "proposal form", "TAT" (turnaround time, they are measured on it, this is your lever).

### Questions, in order

1. "Take the last claim (or proposal) that got stuck. What was missing?"
2. "How does the customer find out what's missing? Who tells them, on what channel?"
3. "How many times does a customer typically send the wrong or unusable document before it's accepted? What's wrong with it usually?"
4. "What percentage of first submissions are usable as-is?" *(the H3 number, get it)*
5. "Do you have a customer portal? What percentage of customers actually use it versus WhatsApp or email or walking in?" *(H1, this is the killer question for them)*
6. "Who raises the requirement, and who accepts it as satisfied? Same person?" *(reviewer separation)*
7. "What's your TAT commitment, and how much of the breach is caused by waiting on the customer rather than by you?"
8. "When a customer has claimed before, do you already hold their KYC? Do you ask again?" *(H4, and the answer is usually yes, they ask again)*
9. "What expires? Agent licences, medical reports, health check validity?" *(H5)*
10. "What happens to your numbers when a case sits pending for 30 days?"
11. Core spine Q25 (H8) and the ask.

### Confirm phrases

- "We keep asking and they keep sending the same blurry photo."
- "Nobody uses the portal, everyone sends it on WhatsApp to the agent."
- "The file was pending for 40 days because of one document."
- "Our TAT is breached mostly because the customer doesn't respond."

### Kill phrases

- "Our system handles requirements end to end and customers respond through the app."
- "The agent handles all of that, we never touch the customer."
- "Legal and IT would never allow documents through a third party tool." *(Not a product kill, but a kill for them as a buyer. Note and downgrade.)*

### The schema question this answers

Insurance has a **third state you have not modelled**: a requirement can be raised, satisfied, and then *re-raised by a downstream reviewer* (underwriter or auditor) after it was already accepted. Ask explicitly: "has a document ever been accepted and then rejected later by someone else?" If yes, your reversible-review decision model is validated and you should note whether reopening after closure needs different recipient copy.

### Your ask

Level 3 (introductions to two agency owners or a TPA contact) is the realistic top of the ladder here. Agency owners and brokers are buyers. Corporate insurance employees are not.

### Trap

They will talk about their internal system the whole time if you let them. Every time they drift into internal software, pull back with: "and on the customer's side, what does the customer actually see?"

## 3.2 Licensing consultants (government licences)

**This is quietly one of the two strongest prospects on your list. Treat it with the seriousness you'd give the CA.**

### Why

Their entire business is: collect documents from a client, assemble a file, submit to a department, get objections, fix, resubmit, receive a licence, then do it again next year at renewal. That is your product's loop, twice over, with money attached. FSSAI, trade licence, shop and establishment, factory licence, pollution board consent, fire NOC, GST registration, MSME, drug licence, IEC.

They also have the thing you want most: **renewals**. Every licence expires. Their client list is a recurring revenue base built on expiry dates, which they currently track in a spreadsheet or in their own head.

### Their vocabulary

"Objection", "query raised", "resubmission", "deficiency memo", "application number", "renewal", "inspector", "portal", "NOC", "affidavit", "self-declaration", "notarised", "documents pending from client".

### Questions, in order

1. "Which licences do you handle most? How many applications last month?"
2. "Take the last one you filed. What did you need from the client, and how many separate items was it?"
3. "How did you ask for it? Show me the message."
4. "How much of it came back right the first time?"
5. "What's the most common thing clients get wrong?" *(listen for: wrong bill, blurry photo, unsigned, outdated, wrong name spelling)*
6. **"Who else besides the client did you need something from?"** *(landlord for the rent agreement, CA for financials, architect for the layout, the electricity bill holder. This is H2, and they will not volunteer it unless you ask)*
7. "Did the department raise an objection? What was it, and how did you handle the loop back to the client?" *(the two-sided rework question, this is unique to them)*
8. "How long between starting and the licence in hand? How much of that was waiting on the client?"
9. "When the same client needs a second licence, what do you ask for again that you already have?" *(H4, the strongest carry-forward evidence available to you)*
10. **"How do you know which client's licence expires next month?"** *(H5. Ask them to show you. The answer is almost always a spreadsheet, and it's almost always out of date)*
11. "What happens if a renewal is missed?" *(penalty, business shutdown, re-application from scratch. Quantify it)*
12. "How many clients do you have on file in total?" *(this is your seat / packet pricing base)*
13. "What do you charge per licence? What do you pay for software today?" *(H7)*
14. Core spine Q25 (H8) and the ask.

### Confirm phrases

- "I've asked him for that PAN card three times, I know I have it somewhere."
- "The department raised an objection and then I had to go back to the client again."
- "I keep a sheet of renewal dates." *(follow up: "when did you last update it?")*
- "The client takes 10 days to send four documents."

### Kill phrases

- "Clients bring everything physically on day one, it's fine."
- "I do 3 or 4 a month." *(volume too low)*
- "I only deal with one client, a big company." *(not a segment)*

### The schema question this answers

**Is the government department a second reviewer in the case, or outside it?** Your model assumes the requester reviews and the case closes. Here, the case closes only when an external authority accepts it. Ask: "if the tool told you the file was complete, would that be enough, or is it only complete when the department accepts it?" If the second, you need a case state for "submitted onward, awaiting external outcome", and the objection loop needs to reopen specific items. That is a real Phase 1.5 decision and this interview is where you learn it.

### Your ask

Push to **level 4**. Script:
> "Next licence file you take on, let me do the client chasing for you. Give me the client's number and the list. I'll get the documents, tell you daily what's missing, and hand you the complete set. Free. One file."

If that works once, you have a paying customer within a month, because their pain is per-file and immediate.

### Trap

They may be chasing 10 clients' documents while talking to you. Use that: "you said you're waiting on three clients right now. Tell me about each one, what's missing and since when." Live pending work is the most honest data you will ever get.

## 3.3 Compliance consultants (BIS, TUV, ISO, CE)

### Why they're on the list

This is your **multi-party stress test**. A single BIS registration or ISO certification pulls documents from the manufacturer (often overseas), a testing laboratory, the brand owner, an authorised Indian representative, and sometimes a trademark attorney. One case, five counterparties, each owing different items, in different time zones, some of whom are not reachable on WhatsApp.

It is also a strong H5 test: certificates carry validity periods and surveillance audits, so the client relationship is recurring by design.

### Their vocabulary

"Scope", "surveillance audit", "recertification", "test report", "lab", "NABL", "AIR" (authorised Indian representative), "factory audit", "non-conformity", "CAPA", "gap analysis", "documentation kit", "apostille", "notarised".

### Questions, in order

1. "Which certifications do you do most? How many active clients right now?"
2. "Take the last certification you closed. List every separate organisation you had to get something from."
3. "Which one is always the slowest? Why?"
4. **"Do you chase each of them separately, or do you send one list to everyone?"** *(the multi-party question, and the answer tells you whether per-party obligations matter)*
5. "When one party is done and another isn't, how do you know? Where do you look?"
6. "What comes back wrong most often? What happens then?"
7. "Do any of these documents come from outside India? How does that change the chasing?" *(channel constraints, email only, timezone, language)*
8. "For a client's second product or second certification, how much of the documentation is the same as the first?" *(H4, and here the answer is usually *most of it*)*
9. "How do you track surveillance audit dates and certificate expiry across all clients?" *(H5, ask to see it)*
10. "What happens to a client if a surveillance audit is missed?"
11. "What do you charge per certification, and how much of that cost is your own team's chasing time?"
12. Core spine Q25 (H8) and the ask.

### Confirm phrases

- "The lab sends the report to the manufacturer and I find out two weeks later."
- "I'm waiting on the factory for photos and on the client for the trademark certificate."
- "Same client, second product, and I ask for the same company documents again."
- "I have a sheet with all the certificate expiry dates."

### Kill phrases

- "It's just one client contact who coordinates everything internally." *(multi-party collapses into single-party: important negative evidence, note it prominently)*
- "Everything goes through email threads and that's fine."

### The schema question this answers

**Is the party or the subject the unit?** In certification, documents attach to a *product*, a *factory*, and a *company*, not to a person. A company document collected for product A should carry forward to product B. That is exactly your `subject_type` / `subject_id` model, and this interview tells you whether the real subjects are companies, factories, products, or all three. Ask directly: "if I asked you 'what do you already hold for this client', would you answer by company, by product, or by factory?"

### Your ask

Level 3 and 4. This profession is small and networked, so introductions compound quickly.

### Trap

They will want to talk about the standards, because that's their expertise and it's interesting. Budget for five minutes of it, then: "that's the technical side. I'm interested in the boring side, the chasing. Who was late last week?"

## 3.4 Bank branch managers

### Set expectations before you start

**A branch manager cannot buy software. Not now, not later.** No branch manager in any bank has that authority. Go in knowing that, and extract the three things they *can* give you:

1. The **objection list** every regulated buyer will throw at you (data residency, outsourcing policy, third party risk, audit trail, information security review). You need to hear these now, from someone who isn't judging you, rather than for the first time in a deal.
2. Evidence for **H1** at scale. Banks have real portals and real net banking, and customers still turn up with a bag of photocopies. That's your thesis, demonstrated by an institution with a billion rupee IT budget.
3. **The recurring-collection use case they own personally**, which is the genuinely valuable part, see below.

### The part most people miss

Ask specifically about **existing borrowers**, not new accounts. Branches must collect, on a schedule, for the life of a loan:

- monthly or quarterly stock and book-debt statements from working capital borrowers
- annual financials and CA-certified statements
- renewed insurance copies for mortgaged or hypothecated assets (expiry, with consequence)
- updated KYC on a periodic cycle
- end-use certificates, unit inspection documents

That is a **recurring, expiry-bearing, penalty-backed collection loop with a named chaser**, and it is the single richest vein in this interview. Most of the pain is real, quantified (penal interest, audit observations, asset classification consequences), and currently run on phone calls and WhatsApp.

### Their vocabulary

"Deficiency", "KYC updation", "stock statement", "renewal", "documentation", "sanction conditions", "post-disbursement documents", "audit observation", "inspection", "NPA", "pendency", "follow-up register".

### Questions, in order

1. "What do you have to collect from existing borrowers on a schedule? Monthly, quarterly, annually?"
2. "Who chases those, and on what? Phone, WhatsApp, letter?"
3. "How many borrowers are pending on any given day?"
4. "What happens if a stock statement is late?" *(penal interest, classification, audit finding. Get the consequence precisely)*
5. "What comes up in audit or inspection about pending documents?" *(this is the internal buyer's pain, remember it)*
6. "For a new account or loan, how much comes in complete the first time?"
7. "Do customers use the bank's own portal or app to send documents? Honestly, what percentage?" *(H1)*
8. "Who in the branch checks the documents versus who collects them?" *(maker-checker, this validates `require_separate_reviewer` directly. Ask: "can the same person do both?" The answer will be a firm no, and that's your enterprise feature justified by a real practitioner)*
9. "If a vendor offered a tool for this, what would happen? Who would have to approve it?" *(let them list the process, write all of it down verbatim, this is your enterprise objection list)*
10. "What are you not allowed to do? Can you receive documents on WhatsApp?" *(the compliance boundary, crucial)*
11. Core spine Q25 (H8) and the ask.

### Confirm phrases

- "I have a register of pending documents and I call them myself."
- "Audit always flags pending documentation."
- "The customer says he'll send it, and then nothing for a month."

### Kill phrases

- "Head office handles all documentation centrally now."

### The schema question this answers

**Maker-checker as a hard requirement, and break-glass audit.** If he confirms that the collector may never be the approver, you have practitioner evidence for `require_separate_reviewer` and for the audited admin access model. That's a section of your security questionnaire answered by a banker rather than by your own reasoning.

### Your ask

Level 3 only, and make it specific:
> "You deal with a lot of CAs, loan agents, and borrowers' finance people. Two of them who spend their day chasing paperwork: can you introduce me?"

**A bank branch manager's referral network is the real prize of this interview.** DSAs, CAs, valuers, and SME finance heads all orbit a branch. Ask for three names, not one.

### Trap

Do not pitch. Do not ask if the bank might use it. You will get a polite "send me a proposal" that means nothing and burns the relationship. This is an insight and referral interview, full stop.

## 3.5 Chartered Accountant (recurring documents)

### Why they're on the list

This is the wedge your review document recommends, and it is the only segment that exercises **recurrence plus carry-forward from the first customer**. Test it properly.

### The shape you're expecting (confirm, don't assume)

Monthly GST cycle: chase sales and purchase data, bank statements, and invoices from 20 to 200 clients, all in the same week, every month, against a statutory deadline with a per-day late fee. Quarterly TDS. Annual ITR and audit season, with prepared-by-client lists running to dozens of items. Chasing is usually done by an article or junior on WhatsApp.

### Their vocabulary

"GSTR-1", "3B", "TDS return", "26AS", "AIS", "bank statement", "purchase register", "Tally backup", "audit", "PBC list", "client not responding", "due date", "late fee", "article", "filing".

### Questions, in order

1. "How many clients, and what do you chase them for each month?"
2. "Walk me through the first week of the month. What actually happens?"
3. "Who does the chasing? An article? How many hours a week does that take them?" *(H6, and get the salary: "what does an article cost you a month?")*
4. "Out of [N] clients, how many send everything on time without a reminder? How many need three or more reminders?" *(the single best number you will get in this interview)*
5. "What comes in wrong?" *(listen for the exact phrase "wrong month", it's your rework hypothesis verbatim)*
6. "How do you tell them it's wrong? And then what?"
7. "How do you know, on the 18th, which clients are still pending?" *(ask them to show you: usually a whiteboard, a notebook, or a junior's memory)*
8. **"Next month you'll ask the same 60 clients for the same things. Does the system know that, or do you start fresh?"** *(H4)*
9. "Is there anything you hold on file that you re-ask for anyway? PAN, GST certificate, previous returns?"
10. "What happens when a client files late? Who pays the late fee, and who gets blamed?" *(this is your cost of delay, and the blame usually lands on the CA, which is a reputational cost worth more than the fee)*
11. "What software do you already pay for? What does it do about client document collection?" *(the bundling risk, asked as a fact question, not a hypothetical)*
12. "Why haven't you used that part of it?" *(if they pay for something that theoretically does this, this question is the most important one in the interview)*
13. "What do you spend a year on software, total?" *(H7 and your price ceiling)*
14. Core spine Q25 (H8) and the ask. **For CAs, H8 is the whole game:** can they force clients onto a link, or will they always accept WhatsApp photos?

### Confirm phrases

- "They sent last month's statement again."
- "I have an article whose entire job in the first week is following up."
- "I keep a sheet of who has given what."
- "Every month, the same 15 clients, the same chasing."

### Kill phrases

- "My clients just send it, I don't really chase." *(they are lying or they have 8 clients, check the count)*
- "TaxDome / Zoho / [practice software] already does this and my clients use it." *(bundling kill. Take it seriously, ask to see it)*
- "The article does it, it's not really a cost." *(the cheap-labour objection. This is the real economic threat to this wedge in India, and you must hear how often it comes up. Note it every single time)*

### The cheap-labour objection: how to handle it

In India, a junior chasing clients costs ₹10,000 to ₹25,000 a month, which makes ₹2,000 a month software feel unnecessary. Do not argue. Probe the parts labour cannot fix:
> "When the article leaves, who knows what's pending?"
> "When you're asked on the 19th which clients are pending, how long does it take to find out?"
> "How many late fees did you eat last year because of a client who didn't send on time?"

The sale to a CA is **visibility and the deadline risk**, not labour cost. Confirm or kill that in the room.

### The schema question this answers

**Is the case per client per period, or one rolling case per client?** You currently model a case as an instance. Ask: "would you want one file per client per month, or one continuous file per client that just keeps going?" Their answer determines whether recurring cases or a rolling ledger view is the right Phase 1.5 shape.

### Your ask

Push hard to **level 4**, timed to their cycle:
> "Next month's GST week: pick five clients you always have to chase. Give me their names and numbers. I'll chase them for you, free, and tell you every day who's pending. You do nothing different."

If they say yes, you have a live concierge pilot with a hard deadline attached, which is the best possible test environment.

### Trap

CAs are busy and will answer in generalities to save time. Anchor everything to a date: "on the 8th of last month, what did you actually do?"

## 3.6 Logistics and transport company COO

### Why they're on the list

Biggest potential cheque, and the best test of the **subject-centric ledger** you have already modelled but not built. They collect documents about *vehicles* and *drivers* and *fleet owners*, not about cases. Documents expire with legal consequence (a detained truck costs money per day). And they have a COO who can actually approve spend.

Note what the owner told you: their cases are "recurring plus evolving, or just one time." That's the most product-relevant sentence anyone has said to you so far. **Evolving means the case never closes**, because a fleet owner keeps adding vehicles and drivers, each of which drags in a new set of documents. Your model has no such object. Find out if that's real.

### The shape you're expecting (confirm, don't assume)

- **Fleet owner / attached vehicle onboarding:** PAN, GST, cancelled cheque, agreement, vehicle RC, permit, fitness, insurance, PUC, driver licence, police verification
- **Recurring expiry:** permits, fitness certificates, insurance, PUC, national permit, driver licence renewals
- **Per-trip:** LR copies, e-way bills, proof of delivery from drivers or consignees, weighment slips, damage photos
- **Client side:** KYC, rate contracts, credit documents
- **Claims:** damaged goods documentation from the consignee

### Their vocabulary

"Attached vehicle", "fleet owner", "LR", "POD", "e-way bill", "permit", "fitness", "PUC", "challan", "detention", "consignee", "indent", "vendor code", "driver badge".

### Questions, in order

1. "What are the different kinds of paperwork you collect from outside the company? Walk me through each."
2. "Which of those is the most painful right now?" *(let them pick, then reconstruct that one in full)*
3. **"When you onboard a new fleet owner, what do you need, from whom, and how long does it take?"** *(H2: owner, driver, and the vehicle each carry documents)*
4. "How many vehicles and drivers in the network? How many are added a month?" *(scale number, drives pricing)*
5. **"How do you know today which vehicle has a permit or fitness expiring this month?"** *(H5. Ask to see the sheet. Sit in silence while they look for it)*
6. "What happened the last time one expired and nobody noticed?" *(detention, fine, penalty, lost trip. Get the rupee number, it will be large and it is your best ROI line in this entire playbook)*
7. "Who owns keeping those documents current? Is it one person?" *(H6)*
8. "PODs: how do they come back? Photos on WhatsApp?" *(high volume, low value per item, but it's a real chasing loop)*
9. "When a fleet owner adds a fifth truck next month, what happens? Do you start a new process or add to their file?" *(the evolving-case question, ask it exactly like this)*
10. "Who inside checks these documents, and who just collects them?"
11. "If a customer audits you, or an insurer does, what do you have to produce and how long does it take?" *(the packet export use case, and it's often a real, painful, recurring event)*
12. "What do you spend on software today? Who approves a new tool?" *(H7, and here the answer can be yes)*
13. Core spine Q25 (H8) and the ask.

### Confirm phrases

- "There's an Excel with all the expiry dates, someone updates it."
- "The truck got stopped because the permit had lapsed."
- "The fleet owner sends photos on WhatsApp and half are unreadable."
- "Every time they add a vehicle we have to collect all over again."

### Kill phrases

- "We have a TMS / ERP that handles vendor master and document expiry." *(probe hard: does it chase, or just store? Storing is not chasing)*
- "It's all handled at the depot level, I wouldn't know." *(wrong person, ask for the right one)*

### The schema question this answers

**Does the case model survive contact with a subject that outlives every case?** A vehicle accumulates documents across years and cases. If this COO describes a permanent per-vehicle and per-driver record, your `LedgerSubject` and `DocumentValidity` design is validated by a real operation, and Phase 1.5 is arguably more urgent than Phase 1b. Ask directly: "would you rather see this organised by request, or by truck?"

### Your ask

Level 4, and the natural scope is one fleet owner onboarding or one batch of expiry renewals:
> "Give me your list of vehicles with documents expiring in the next 60 days. I'll chase the owners myself and hand you back the current documents. Free, one batch."

That is a spectacular demo of value with zero product required.

### Trap

This process is big, and you will get lost. Force a single lane in minute five: "of everything you've described, pick the one that costs you the most, and let's only talk about that one for the next 20 minutes."

## 3.7 Freelance HR (hiring for multiple companies)

### Why they're on the list

Not as a buyer. She is a freelancer, willing to pay will be low, and your own review document is right that the freelancer segment is a weak market. She is on this list for one specific reason: **she is the only person on it who runs many separate clients' confidential cases from one desk**, which is the exact scenario behind your `restricted` access mode, your workspace boundary question, and your dormant `Organization` table.

She also tests H2 well (candidate, previous employer, background verification vendor, client company) and H3 well (documents come back as blurry WhatsApp photos, constantly).

### Their vocabulary

"Offer", "joining", "DOJ" (date of joining), "BGV" (background verification), "relieving letter", "experience letter", "payslips", "offer drop", "ghosting", "onboarding docket", "candidate dropped out".

### Questions, in order

1. "How many clients are you hiring for right now? How many candidates in flight?"
2. "Between offer and joining day, what do you have to collect from a candidate? List everything."
3. "Who else do you need something from? The previous employer? A verification agency? The client?" *(H2)*
4. "Take the last candidate who nearly missed their joining date. What happened?"
5. "What comes back wrong? How do you handle it?" *(expect: photos, cropped documents, wrong file, missing page)*
6. **"How do you keep client A's candidate files away from client B?"** *(this is THE question for you. Ask what happens today. The answer is probably "separate WhatsApp groups and separate Drive folders", which is exactly the access boundary you're designing)*
7. "Have you ever sent something to the wrong client by mistake, or nearly?" *(let the silence sit)*
8. "If a candidate you placed last year applies through you again, do you still have their documents? Do you ask again?" *(H4)*
9. "Do your client companies give you their own system to use, or do you use your own?" *(this decides whether your buyer is her or the company)*
10. "Does anything you collect expire or need refreshing?" *(usually weak here, note it)*
11. "Do you pay for any tool today? What?" *(H7, expect small)*
12. Core spine Q25 (H8), phrased for her: "if a candidate says he'll just WhatsApp you the documents, what do you do?"

### Confirm phrases

- "I have a separate WhatsApp group per client and I get confused."
- "The candidate ghosted after accepting and I had to tell the client."
- "I chase the same five documents from every single candidate."
- "The client keeps asking me what's pending."

### Kill phrases

- "The client's HR system handles all onboarding documents, I just hand over the candidate." *(if this is universal, her use case collapses and she's just a design partner)*
- "I do 2 or 3 hires a month." *(volume too low to matter commercially, though still useful for the access-model question)*

### The schema question this answers

**Is a workspace per client company needed, or is a `restricted` case enough?** Ask it in her language: "if a tool showed you all your candidates in one place, would it be a problem that all your clients' candidates are in one list? Or would you need them completely walled off?" Her answer directly decides whether you need `Organization` and multi-workspace earlier than planned, or whether per-case access mode covers it.

Second question worth asking: "when a client asks you for a status update, what do you send them?" If the answer is "a screenshot or a message", that's the read-only Viewer role validated by a real person, and it supports your free Viewer seat idea.

### Your ask

Level 2 and 3. She is a design partner, not a customer. Say so honestly, it will make her more useful:
> "Honestly, I don't think you're who I'd sell this to. But your setup is the messiest version of the problem and that's exactly what I need to see. Can I watch you do the next one?"

People are far more helpful when they aren't being sold to, and she may well introduce you to HR heads at her client companies, who **are** buyers.

### Trap

She's warm and will be encouraging. Encouragement is noise. Every time she says something positive, ask for a specific instance of the opposite: "when did it last go badly?"

# PART 4: MORE PEOPLE WORTH REACHING OUT TO

Ranked by (pain × can decide alone × you can reach them this week). Each has one **qualifying question** you can ask on the phone in 60 seconds to decide whether a full interview is worth it.

## Tier A: add at least two of these to Round 1

### A1. Loan DSAs, mortgage and business loan agents

Possibly the highest-pain group in this entire document. A single home loan file needs documents from the applicant, the co-applicant, the employer, the builder, and sometimes a guarantor. The bank rejects the file for one missing page, and the DSA's commission is delayed or lost. Money consequence is immediate, personal, and large.

- **Tests:** H2 (strongly), H3 (strongly), H6, H7, H8
- **Qualifying question:** "How many files are you carrying right now where you're waiting on the customer for documents?"
- **Why they can buy:** self-employed, decide alone, already spend on lead generation tools.
- **Where to find them:** every bank branch has a list, and your branch manager interview should produce two names.

### A2. Immigration, visa, and study-abroad consultants

Student, parent, sponsor, university, and bank, all owing different documents against a hard intake deadline. Rework is constant (wrong format, wrong certification, missing attestation). Revenue per client is high, so software budget exists.

- **Tests:** H2, H3, H4 (per intake cycle), H8
- **Qualifying question:** "In an intake season, how many applications are you running at once, and how many are stuck on documents right now?"

### A3. Customs house agents and freight forwarders

Per-shipment document packs from importers and exporters, with the cleanest quantifiable cost of delay available anywhere: demurrage and detention charges accrue per container per day. Recurring per client, forever.

- **Tests:** H3, H4, H6, and the best cost-of-delay number you will find
- **Qualifying question:** "When a shipment is held because a document is missing, what does a day cost?"

### A4. Payroll and labour compliance firms (PF, ESIC, PT, returns)

Monthly, statutory, penalty-backed, across many client companies. Structurally almost identical to the CA wedge but less crowded with bundled software.

- **Tests:** H4 (strongly), H6, H8
- **Qualifying question:** "How many client companies do you file for monthly, and how many are late because of the client?"

### A5. Property managers, rental agencies, and real estate documentation services

Tenant onboarding (KYC, agreement, police verification, employer letter), owner documentation, and sale-side legal clearance packs pulled from owner, buyer, bank, and society. Recurring, and every file is multi-party.

- **Tests:** H2, H3, H5 (agreements and police verification expire)
- **Qualifying question:** "For one rental deal, how many different people do you have to collect papers from?"
- **Note:** you already operate in this space through Draazy, so you have a captive first case and a dogfooding path nobody else on this list gives you. Use your own rental and clearance files as concierge pilot number one, and be honest with yourself that your own usage is not market evidence.

## Tier B: worth one interview each, later

| Who | Why | Qualifying question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Statutory auditors** | PBC lists are your product in spreadsheet form. Overlaps the CA, so only do this if the CA interview goes well | "How many open items are on your current PBC list, and how do you track them?" |
| **Tender and bid managers (construction, infra)** | One bid assembled from many subcontractors and internal teams, hard deadline, disqualification if incomplete | "What's the last bid you nearly missed because a document didn't arrive?" |
| **Hospital insurance desks and TPAs** | Enormous document chasing volume, but an enterprise sale | "Who chases the patient for missing claim documents?" |
| **NGO grant and CSR compliance** | Utilisation certificates, audited statements, recurring per donor, donor acts as the external reviewer | "How many donor reports are pending documents right now?" |
| **Franchise onboarding managers** | Structured, recurring, expiry-bearing, and a real company budget | "How long from signing a franchisee to them being fully documented?" |
| **School and college admissions offices** | Massive seasonal volume, very low budget | "How many applicants are incomplete two weeks before the deadline?" |

## Tier C: skip for now

Freelancers, agencies, interior designers, wedding and event vendors. Your own review document already reached the right conclusion here. Low urgency, low willingness to pay, and free tools genuinely work for them.

## The saturation rule

Stop adding professions at 10. Breadth past that is procrastination wearing a lab coat. The point of Round 1 is to **eliminate**, and if after 10 interviews you cannot name one profession you'd bet on, the honest read is that the pain is not sharp enough anywhere you can reach, which is itself a finding worth two months of your life.

Two derived columns to compute at the end:

## 5.3 Gate 1: after interview 5

Stop. Do not book more until you have written one page answering:

1. Which two hypotheses are getting the strongest unprompted evidence? Which is getting none?
2. Which profession produced the highest **buyability** score?
3. What did every single person say, regardless of profession? *(that's your positioning sentence, and it will be in their words, not yours)*
4. What did I assume that turned out to be wrong?
5. Which three questions should be cut, and which three added?

Then rewrite your question list. An interview list that hasn't changed by number 5 means you aren't listening.

## 5.4 Gate 2: after interview 8 to 10, pick the vertical

Choose on this rule, in this order:

1. **Buyability first.** One person can decide, and they will enforce it on their counterparties. If H7 or H8 is zero for a profession, it is out, no matter how much pain it has.
2. **Frequency second.** 10+ cases a month, and recurring.
3. **Cost of a stalled case third.** If they cannot name a consequence worth roughly ₹3,000 or more per case, willingness to pay is thin.
4. **Structural fit last.** Does their world need multi-party, rework, or carry-forward? This decides whether your existing architecture is an advantage or just overhead.

Write the choice down as a sentence, with the backup:
> "We are launching on ______________, because ______________. If it fails, the backup is ______________."

Then **stop interviewing everyone else** and run 15 to 20 more in that one profession, per your own kill condition.

## 5.5 Turning interviews into pilots

Two weeks after Gate 2, you should be running concierge pilots, not writing code. The sequence:

1. Pick the 5 people from Round 1 and 2 who reached ask level 3 or higher.
2. Offer level 4 to all 5 (manual chasing, one real case, free).
3. Run them **by hand**, using WhatsApp and email and a spreadsheet. Do not put them into your half-built product. Your goal is to learn the loop, not to demo the software.
4. After one completed case each, ask for money for the next cycle. The script:

> "That one's done. Next month I'll charge ₹____ for it. Same thing, same result, you do nothing different. Yes or no?"

1. Three yeses clears your kill condition. Fewer than three, and you either change the vertical or you stop.

Charge from the second case. Never the first. And never say "it's free forever while I build", because then you never learn anything about price.

# PART 6: WHAT SHOULD MAKE YOU STOP OR TURN

Print this. Re-read it at Gate 1 and Gate 2. Confirmation bias is the default state of anyone who has already written code.

## Stop signals (any two of these in one profession means drop that profession)

- Under 5 relevant cases a month.
- Nobody can name what a stalled case costs.
- The chasing is done by someone so cheap that nobody cares.
- "If the client insists on WhatsApp, I take it on WhatsApp." (H8 failure)
- Every single case has exactly one counterparty and four items.
- The feature is already bundled into software they cannot leave.
- They like the demo but will not move one real case into it.

## Signals you are interviewing badly (fix the interviewer, not the idea)

- People compliment the idea and you feel good afterwards.
- You talked for more than a third of the time.
- You cannot write down three verbatim quotes.
- Everyone agrees with you.
- You revealed the product in the first 10 minutes.
- You promised someone a feature.

## The two findings you should actively hope for

1. **"My cases are single-party, one list, no rework."** If several professions say this, your three structural bets are wrong, and finding that out for the cost of seven conversations is the cheapest thing that will ever happen to you.
2. **A profession nobody on this list predicted.** The best wedge frequently arrives as an offhand remark: "you should talk to my friend who does X, he's drowning." Follow those. They are the highest-yield lead you will get, and they are free.

## Turn signals (change the product, not the market)

- Everyone organises by **subject** (vehicle, client, vendor) rather than by request. → Phase 1.5 ledger moves up the priority list.
- Everyone has an **external reviewer** downstream (a department, an authority, a bank, a donor). → your case model needs an "awaiting external outcome" state, and that is a real gap.
- Everyone's case **never closes**, it just keeps growing. → your case-as-instance model needs a rolling variant.
- Nobody cares about signatures. → your Phase 2 slides further right, which is good news for your timeline.

# APPENDIX A: OUTREACH MESSAGES

## A1. Cold outreach (WhatsApp or email), for the ones who don't know you yet

> Hi [name], I'm [your name] from [city]. I'm doing research on how [profession] actually collect paperwork and information from clients, and the honest version of it, not what's written online. I'm not selling anything and I have nothing to show you. Would you give me 30 minutes this week or next? I'll come to you, or a call works. Happy to share what I learn from everyone else I'm speaking with, which is usually the more useful part for you.

Why it works: no product, no pitch, an explicit low ask, and one thing offered back that costs you nothing and is genuinely valuable (a summary of how their peers do it).

## A2. Referral request, at the end of every interview

> "This was genuinely useful, thank you. One last thing: who else do you know doing this same work, that I should talk to? If you can introduce me to two of them over WhatsApp, that's the biggest help you can give me."

Then sit quietly while they think. Do not fill the silence with "no pressure."

## A3. Follow-up, same day

> Thanks again for the time. The thing that stuck with me was [one specific thing they said, in their words]. I hadn't expected that. I'll send you what I learn across all these conversations once I'm done. And if you do get stuck chasing a file in the next few weeks, tell me, I'm happy to do the chasing for you as a test of what I'm building.

That last line plants the level 4 ask without making it a pitch, and it converts at a surprising rate a week or two later.

# APPENDIX B: THE 60-SECOND REVEAL (only when they ask twice)

Say this, do not read it:

> "One link per [their word for the counterparty]. They never make an account and never get a password. They open it on their phone and see only what's still missing. When they send something wrong, you reject it with a reason, and only that one item reopens, and the reason goes into the next reminder automatically. Next cycle, it doesn't re-ask for anything you already hold that's still valid. And at any moment you can see which [counterparty] is blocking which file."

Then stop and ask one of these, never both:

- "Where does that break for you?"
- "What's missing from that for it to be usable in your office?"

Then write down the answer without defending anything.

If you show the screen: recipient page first, on a phone, not the requester dashboard. The recipient page is your strongest asset and the requester dashboard doesn't exist yet.

# APPENDIX C: THE ONE-PAGE CHEAT SHEET

Carry only this into the room.

**Opening:** "I'm not selling anything. Walk me through the last real one, start to finish."

**The spine:** trigger → what you asked for → who you asked → how you sent it → what came back wrong → how many follow-ups → who chased → where it ended up → how you knew it was done → what it repeats → what expires → what it cost.

**Always get:** cases/month · parties/case · % right first time · who chases · what lateness costs · what they pay for software today.

**Always ask:** "If they refuse and send it on WhatsApp anyway, what do you do?"

**Always end with:** an ask. Level 4 if there's any warmth at all.

**Never:** pitch first · fix objections live · promise features · accept a compliment as data · ask what they would do.

**Three seconds of silence after they finish.** Always.
